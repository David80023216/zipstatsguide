Frequently Asked Questions

Our data is sourced from the US Census Bureau's American Community Survey (ACS) 5-Year Estimates and other public federal datasets. We update annually as new data becomes available.
The data reflects the most recent Census survey estimates, typically from 2022–2023 surveys. Actual current figures may vary slightly as populations and economics change.
Median household income is the middle value of all household incomes in a ZIP code — meaning half of households earn more and half earn less. It's a better measure than average because it's not skewed by very high earners.
Some rural or low-population ZIP codes may have limited Census survey responses, resulting in less precise estimates. The Census suppresses data for very small populations to protect privacy.
Yes! The underlying Census data is public domain. Our aggregated display is free for personal, research, and business use. Please cite 'US Census Bureau / ZIP Stats Guide' when publishing.
If you're unsure of your ZIP code, check any piece of mail delivered to your address, or search USPS.com for ZIP code lookup tools.
A cost of living index compares the relative expense of living in an area to the national average (index = 100). An index of 120 means living costs are 20% higher than average; 85 means 15% lower.
Median home value is the midpoint of all home values in a ZIP code — half of homes are worth more and half are worth less. It reflects owner-occupied housing and is based on Census self-reported estimates.
